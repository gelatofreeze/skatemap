# Deep-research prompt: making Frutiger Air the best map it can be

Paste everything below the line into a new Claude session with web search
(deep research mode if available). It is written to stand alone.

---

You are researching for a design decision, not writing an essay. Run deep
research: many searches, primary sources first, cross-check every number
against a second source, and say plainly when something is a guess.

## The project

I am building **Frutiger Air**, a custom map for EA's *skate.* (the 2025
free-to-play game by Full Circle), loaded through the **ReSkate** mod
platform. The map is generated by Python code that drives Blender, then
compiled with ReSkate Studio. Because it is code, every finding you give
me can become a number: a radius, a gap length, a spacing, a colour.

What exists now: a 400 m tropical island in a lagoon. A glass tower in
the centre with mega ramps running from a 45 m deck to the sea (a classic
roll-in and gap, a full loop, a launch over water, a glass full-pipe
through an aquarium). A terraced street plaza, bowls, mini ramps, a
boardwalk ring. Planned next: an 85 m hollow tower with a near-vertical
drop that launches over the lagoon, a 950 m mega highway with rollers and
jumps that U-turns around a second island, far denser street spots, and
skateable retro-desktop objects (window billboards as wallrides, a
taskbar ledge, cascading error dialogs as a stair set).

The look is **Frutiger Aero** pushed as far as it goes: gloss, glass,
aqua and lime, bubbles, caustic water, tropical fish, white eco
architecture, auroras, early-2000s interface chrome.

Hard limits you must respect:
- Geometry is static. No moving parts, no triggers built into the map.
- ReSkate plugins (Luau) can read skater telemetry, draw PNG/JPG pictures
  and shapes in the world, play short WAV sounds and route the player's
  music to a speaker placed in the world, and make small HTTPS requests.
  They cannot play video.
- The game's physics constants are not published. Treat any number you
  find as unverified unless it comes from measurement.
- Players arrive with zero instructions. The map must teach itself.

## What I need to know

Answer each section with findings, the evidence behind them, and a
confidence level (measured / well-sourced / community consensus / guess).

**1. How this game actually rides.**
Top rolling speed, push speed, gravity feel, how much speed a slope
gives and how fast it bleeds on flat, pumping, how the skater behaves on
transitions, vert, loops and full-pipes, grind and manual detection, what
makes the skater bail, known collision quirks in custom maps. Search the
ReSkate docs (docs.reskate.dev), the ReSkate Discord summaries, GitHub
issues, Thunderstore map pages and their comments, skatemods.com, Reddit,
and YouTube videos of mega ramps and custom maps in this game. Compare
with Skate 3 where the series' behaviour is better documented, and say
where the 2025 game differs.

**2. What makes a skate map get played again and again.**
Study the most-downloaded ReSkate maps and the most-loved parks in
Skate 3, Skater XL, Session and the Tony Hawk series. What do the best
ones share? Line density, sight lines, how spots chain, run-out design,
how long before a new player finds a line that feels good, what players
complain about. Then real-world skatepark design practice: transition
radii and heights by skill level, ledge and rail heights, stair
dimensions, flat-bottom lengths, spacing between obstacles, flow-park and
pump-track geometry, and real MegaRamp and big-air dimensions.

**3. The reward science, applied honestly.**
What does research say about why movement games feel good? Flow theory
and challenge-skill balance, variable reward and near-miss effects,
competence and autonomy in self-determination theory, "juice" and game
feel, speed perception and field of view, risk-reward pacing, the value
of landmarks and of visible goals, completion and collection drives,
session length and the "one more try" loop. For each, give the finding,
how strong the evidence is, and a concrete way to build it into static
geometry, sight lines, colour or optional plugin feedback. Separate
well-replicated results from pop-science. I want players to feel great,
not to be manipulated: skip anything that works by frustrating people.

**4. Frutiger Aero, done properly.**
Trace the aesthetic to its sources (Windows Vista and 7 Aero, Mac OS X
Aqua, Wii and Xbox 360 dashboards, mid-2000s stock imagery, eco-futurist
architecture, the Consumer Aesthetics Research Institute's definition).
List its motifs in order of how strongly they signal the style, its real
palettes with hex values, its materials and lighting, its type, and its
neighbours (Frutiger Eco, Frutiger Metro, Technozen, Dorfic, Y2K, Aqua)
so I can tell what belongs and what does not. Find what makes renditions
look cheap or wrong. Then: how do you make it read in a real-time 3D game
with no ray tracing, and how do you keep a skate surface readable when
everything is glossy and bright?

**5. Wayfinding and friendliness.**
How should a 400 m island teach a new player where to go with no text?
Colour-coding lines, landmark hierarchy, spawn placement, difficulty
gradients, how to recover after a bail, accessibility for colour-blind
players, performance on weak PCs and Steam Deck (triangle and texture
budgets that custom ReSkate maps survive on).

## What to hand back

1. A one-page summary: the ten changes that would improve this map most,
   ranked, each with the evidence behind it.
2. A **parameter table** I can paste into code: recommended values and
   safe ranges for speeds, gap lengths per metre of drop, transition
   radii, ledge and rail heights, obstacle spacing, line lengths, colour
   values, texture and triangle budgets. Mark each as measured, sourced
   or estimated.
3. A **spot list**: at least forty specific obstacles or lines worth
   adding, each with dimensions, where it fits, and which finding it
   serves. Include skateable retro-interface objects.
4. A list of things that would break the experience, so I can avoid them.
5. Open questions that only in-game testing can settle, each with the
   test that would settle it.
6. Full source list with links.

Do not pad. If the evidence on a point is thin, say so in one line and
move on.
